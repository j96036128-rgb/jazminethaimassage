Choosing a massage

Thai massage or deep tissue: which one for a stiff back?

Both are firm, but they work in different ways. Here is how to choose.

A stiff back is the most common reason people book with us, and the most common question is which massage to have for it. Thai massage and deep tissue are both firm treatments, but they go about it in different ways.

How Thai massage works on stiffness

Thai massage combines pressure along the muscles with assisted stretches. The therapist moves your hips, legs, shoulders and back through a sequence of positions, so the whole body is worked instead of one spot. It is usually done clothed and without oil. People who feel stiff all over, or who sit for most of the day, often prefer it because they come away feeling they can move more freely.

How deep tissue works on stiffness

Deep tissue massage is done with oil, using slow strokes and sustained pressure on the areas that feel knotted. There is little or no stretching. It suits a specific tight area, such as between the shoulder blades, the lower back or the neck, where you want the therapist to stay and work for longer.

A simple way to choose

If the stiffness is general and you want to feel looser, try Thai massage. If you can point to the spot, try deep tissue. If you are not sure, tell us where it is tight and what you do day to day, and we will suggest one. Many regulars alternate between the two.

How firm should it be?

Either treatment should feel like a good ache and never sharp. Say so if the pressure is too much or not enough. It is normal to feel a little tender the next day after a firm session.

When to see someone else first

Massage is for tight, tired muscles. Back pain that is severe, came on suddenly, travels down a leg, comes with numbness or tingling, or has not eased after a few weeks should be checked by your GP or a physiotherapist before you book.
