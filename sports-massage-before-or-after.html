Sports massage for footballers and runners: before or after?

Both have a place, but they are different treatments and the timing matters.

It is one of the questions we are asked most by people who play Sunday league, run parkruns or train at the gym: should I have a sports massage before the game or after it? The answer is that both have a place, but they are different treatments and the timing matters.

In the days before

A sports massage two to four days before a match or race is the most popular choice. It gives the therapist time to work firmly on tight areas, typically calves, hamstrings, hips and lower back, and gives your body time to settle afterwards. A deep treatment the day before, or on the morning of a game, is usually not a good idea, as firm work can leave muscles feeling heavy for a day or so.

On the day

If you want something on match day, keep it short and light. A brief, brisk massage is used by many athletes as part of a warm-up, but it is not a substitute for warming up properly.

After the game

A gentler massage in the day or two after hard exercise is what most people find helps them feel recovered. It is a good time to mention anything that felt tight or sore while you were playing, so the therapist can spend longer there.

Regular treatments

People who train every week often find that a massage every three or four weeks suits them better than one long session when something already hurts. It also means your therapist gets to know where you tend to tighten up.

When to see someone else first

Massage is for tired and tight muscles. If you have a sudden sharp pain, swelling, a suspected tear or strain, or pain that is not improving, see a physiotherapist or your GP before booking. Tell your therapist about any injury, however minor it seems.
